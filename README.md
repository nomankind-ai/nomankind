# nomankind

nomankind checks where a fact came from before an AI model learns it, and keeps the proof.

Models learn from the world, and every fact they take in came from somewhere. Today that somewhere is usually figured out later, if at all: audits try to trace what a model was trained on after the weights already hold it, and mostly they cannot. nomankind works the other way around. Before a fact can be learned from, its source is captured and hashed, three independent operators check it and sign, and the record is sealed with a timestamp. Only then is it offered to a model. Proof first, use second.

It starts with one area, the AI ecosystem, because that is where models fall behind fastest. A model is frozen at its training cutoff, but prices, rate limits, model behavior, and APIs keep changing. nomankind is a running, cited record of those changes: an append-only log of small facts, each verified by three operators who are neither the submitter nor a model provider, each hashed and sealed so any edit shows, and each dated so you can see how fresh it is.

Built on the [1F916 protocol](https://1f916.org) for agent identity and sealed logs. Learn more at [nomankind.ai](https://nomankind.ai).

## Primary use: feeding continual learners

The log is built first for models that train from it. A continual learner pulls every change since its last sync as a sealed delta stream, in the exact order it was sealed, so two models syncing from the same position take in the same sequence and can prove it. Facts that were overturned travel as explicit unlearn signals. Each fact carries its evidence and a last-confirmed date, so a learner can weight it, hold it, or skip it. A drift attestation lets independent operators certify in public that a model's beliefs still match the record. Every fact a learner takes from the stream arrives with its chain of custody complete: source hash, three signatures, seal time, reproduction counts where a predicate exists, and every dispute since. This is provenance of the slice a model learned from the log, not of its training set.

## Verifiable now, and empirical where it can be

Verified means three independent operators confirmed that the source says what the entry says (two while the trusted pool is still under ten operators), and the entry stays open to dispute forever. For a fact that rests only on a cited page, that is provenance, and the log says so. Anyone can check that offline with two files and one script: `npm run verify -- <entry.json> <log.json>` prints the schema version it checked against, then exits 0 when the entry checks out and 1 with the named diffs when it does not.

The application itself runs as a Cloudflare Worker with the log and everything derived from it in D1. `npm run dev` serves it locally, with a health endpoint at `/health` that reports whether the Worker booted and whether its database answers. Schema changes are numbered SQL files under `migrations/`, applied in name order by wrangler and never edited after merge.

Every merge to main deploys the demo environment at demo.nomankind.ai through the Deploy demo action, which typechecks, runs the tests, applies the D1 migrations, and only then puts the Worker live. An annotated tag `v<major>.<minor>.<patch>` on main deploys production at app.nomankind.ai, and the apex nomankind.ai, through the Deploy production action. Every pull request gets a preview version of the demo environment, and a comment carrying its URL, or the uploaded version id when Cloudflare generates no preview URL because the Worker implements a Durable Object. The actions need two repository secrets, `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID` and `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN`; no deploy is ever run by hand.

Verification is the floor. Every entry carries an evidence tier in its signed core. A stated entry rests on a document. An observed entry rests on a measurement: a metered price call, a probe to a rate limit, an endpoint returning its deprecation error, a reproduced model behavior. The submitter freezes the test and its receipt with the claim, validators first judge whether the test decides the claim and then run it themselves under a published n-of-k rule, and each records its own receipt. Behavior and misbehavior entries are observed by rule. Observed entries earn a larger read share, so the operators who measure are paid more than the operators who copy. That is the path from verification toward truth: where a claim can be measured, the record moves past "a source said it" toward "this was observed to hold," fact by fact. Where it cannot, the entry stays stated and honest about it. The tier tells a reader which kind of entry they are holding. A confidence field derived from the receipts is planned; it stays null until there is enough dispute history to calibrate it, and its raw inputs are exposed in the meantime.

## Also for frozen models

A model that reads at inference time gets the fastest true fact on wake: one signed entry with a receipt, no vendor page and no injection surface.

## The record outlives the source

Sources rot. Labs edit their own documentation quietly, pages move, and the page a fact came from can be changed or taken down. nomankind captures what a source said at the moment it was cited, hashes it, and seals it into a witnessed log. Even if the original page is later edited or destroyed, the sealed, dated, independently verified record of what it said still stands, and anyone can check it offline. A legal takedown can remove a served copy of a page, but not the hash, the signatures, or the proof of what it once said.

That guarantee reaches well past the AI ecosystem. The mechanism generalizes to any domain with checkable predicates, and degrades to provenance-only where they do not exist: who said it, what it said at capture, who confirmed it, when it was sealed. Take a work of art: if the piece is lost or destroyed, that same record, who made it, what it was, and who independently vouched for it, still stands on its own. nomankind keeps its scope narrow, the AI ecosystem, by design. New domains come only after this one is saturated.

The operator registry is served by the Worker: `POST /operators` to join, `POST /genesis` for the maintainer's one-time naming of the first trusted operators, and `GET /operators`, `GET /operators/{id}` and `GET /agents/{id}` to read the result. Joining takes the whitepaper's three steps — publish a TXT record at `_nomankind.<your domain>` carrying your 1F916 agent id, complete payout onboarding, and sign the independence attestation for the domain you are joining with your 1F916 key — and the DNS check is real everywhere, while payout onboarding is mocked on demo until the payment provider is wired. The registration stores the connected account id the body carried as the operator's payout reference (D-053: that id and nothing else about the account), which is what a payout cycle transfers against; operators registered before this was stored carry no reference and are skipped by the payout step until they re-register. The registration body carries the registered `domain` the operator joins first, and the attestation it signs is that domain's own sentence under that domain's version, both read out of the domain registry; a body naming no domain reads as `ai-ecosystem`, which is what a client written before schema v0.7 meant. `GET /operators/{id}` lists the `domains` the operator is attested in, and `POST /operators/{id}/domains` takes on a later one: the body is `{domain, attestation}`, signed by one of the operator's own agents, and the refusals are 400 `bad_id` or `bad_body`, 401 for a bad request signature, 404 `not_found`, 403 `agent_mismatch` when the signing key answers for somebody else, then `unregistered_operator`, `unregistered_domain`, `excluded_party` (403), `already_joined` (409), `missing_attestation`, `bad_attestation` and `attestation_domain_mismatch`. Eligibility follows the attestation: an operator judges, reconfirms, requests a check of, or scores an attestation in a domain only where it has signed that domain's sentence, and the exclusion rule is that domain's own published party list rather than one global one — so an operator excluded in one domain stays eligible in another. The trusted pool itself stays global. Locally, apply the migrations first with `npx wrangler d1 migrations apply nomankind-local --local`, because `npm run dev` starts on an empty local database and these routes answer 503 `storage_unreachable` until the schema is there.

Entries are submitted to the same Worker. `POST /entries` takes `{entry, receipt}`, where `entry` is the eighteen immutable core keys plus the author's `signature` over their JCS canonical form and `receipt` is the observation receipt object, sent only when the core carries an `observation`. The request itself is signed like every other write: the four `x-nomankind-*` headers carrying the agent id, a timestamp, a single-use nonce, and an Ed25519 signature over the method, the path and the canonical body. Anyone may submit with a bare agent key, and the entry then names no operator; a key bound to a registered operator must name that operator in its signed core. The Worker fetches the citation itself under the norm-v1.2 rule — fixed headers, at most five redirects, a thirty-second timeout, the final URL recorded — and refuses the submission unless the hash of what it fetched is the `snapshot_hash` the author signed. Refusals are 401 for a bad request signature or a bad entry signature, 403 when the signing key is not the author, 409 for an entry already in the log, and 422 for everything the entry itself gets wrong: `snapshot_mismatch`, `needs_javascript` for a page whose content only a browser could show, `invalid_json`, `unsupported_citation`, the fetch refusals (`bad_status`, `too_large`, `too_many_redirects`, `timeout`, `fetch_failed`), a supersession that does not hold, a missing or mismatched receipt, and `schema_invalid` with the failing fields. Three of those refusals are the domain's: `missing_domain` for a seventeen-key core, which is what an entry sealed before schema v0.7 carried and which no new entry may, `unregistered_domain` for a slug the registry does not hold, and `category_not_in_domain` for a category outside that domain's own enum — the schema's category enum is the union of every registered domain's, and which of them a domain admits is the registry document's table. `domain` is part of the signed core rather than a field beside it, so an entry can never be re-homed: the id, the entry hash and the author's signature all cover it. Nothing is written unless every check passes. On success the answer is 201 with the derived entry, which is `draft`: status is recomputed from the log and is never sent in.

`GET /entries/{id}` returns that entry. The raw capture is public too: `GET /captures/{hash}` returns the bytes that were archived under a `snapshot_hash` or a `receipt_hash`, with the archive address in `x-nomankind-archive-hash`, and `GET /captures/{hash}/sidecar` returns the norm rule's `{final_url, status, headers, fetched_at, fetcher}` record of the fetch. A capture is served as evidence rather than as a page — its stored media type, but `Content-Disposition: attachment`, `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff` and `Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'none'; sandbox` — because anyone can submit with a bare key and the bytes are a stranger's. The captures live in R2 under the `CAPTURES` binding — `nomankind-local-captures`, `nomankind-demo-captures`, `nomankind-production-captures` — content addressed and written once, so a capture is evidence rather than a cache.

## Validating, and the demo checkpoint

Decisions come in at `POST /entries/{id}/validate`, signed like every other write. The body is `{record, signature}`: `record` is exactly the schema's `approvers[]` item — agent, operator, decision, reason, snapshot_hash, assigned_random, test_accepted, reproduction, observation, signed_at — and `signature` is the validator's `nomankind-record-v1` signature over the entry id, the kind and the record, which sits beside the record rather than inside it because the schema has no field for it. The validator's own snapshot hash is the point: each validator fetches the live source itself, so the capture taken at submission is never the only witness to what the page said. Status is never sent in and never set: the entry that comes back is derived from a log that now holds this decision. Refusals are 400 `bad_id` or `bad_body`, 401 for a bad request signature, 403 `agent_mismatch` when the key that signed the envelope is not the record's own, 404 `not_found`, 409 `entry_closed` once the verdict stands, and 422 for everything the record itself gets wrong — the exclusions (`submitter_operator`, `maintainer_operator`, `provider_operator`, `duplicate_operator`, `unregistered_agent`, `operator_mismatch`), `missing_snapshot_hash`, `missing_reason`, `assigned_random_without_assignment`, `bad_signed_at`, `bad_record_signature`, the evidence-shape refusals, and `schema_invalid` with the failing fields. Nothing is written unless every check passes. A superseding entry names its target in its own frozen core, and the target's `superseded_by` is written into the target's stored row in the very same batch as the decision that verifies the superseder — never at submission and never on a decision that does not verify — so no reader ever sees a superseding entry verified and its target still standing.

Reconfirmations come in at `POST /entries/{id}/reconfirm`, signed the same way. The body is `{record, signature}` again: `record` is exactly the schema's `reconfirmations[]` item — agent, operator, snapshot_hash, reproduction, observation, signed_at, every key present and the unused ones null — and `signature` is the reconfirmer's `nomankind-record-v1` signature over the entry id, the kind `reconfirmation` and the record. Any trusted operator can refresh a stale entry, and only a trusted one; no agent under the submitter's own operator may, exactly as at validation. The attestation rests on a fresh snapshot hash the reconfirmer took for themselves, plus a fresh reproduction for a behavior or misbehavior entry and a fresh measurement for any other entry that verified as observed. Nothing derived is sent in: the entry that comes back is derived from a log that now holds the attestation, so `last_confirmed` advances to the record's date, the window reopens, and the reconfirmer takes a read-share slot — the oldest one, unless they already hold a slot, in which case the refresh rotates nothing. The bounty the entry accrued while it was stale is written into the ledger in the same batch as the event that earned it, running from the old `expires_at` to the instant it became fresh again, with no amount on it until the pricing is published. Refusals are 400 `bad_id` or `bad_body`, 401 for a bad request signature, 403 `agent_mismatch`, 404 `not_found`, 409 `entry_not_stale` for an entry still inside its window (a check inside the window is a staked revalidation request, below), 422 for everything the record itself gets wrong — `entry_not_verified`, `unregistered_agent`, `operator_mismatch`, `submitter_agent`, `submitter_operator`, `untrusted_operator`, `missing_snapshot_hash`, the reproduction and observation shape refusals, `bad_signed_at`, `bad_record_signature` — and `schema_invalid` with the failing fields. Nothing is written unless every check passes.

`GET /events` pages the whole log: `?after=<seq>&limit=<n>` resumes strictly after a position the caller already has and answers `{events, head}`, so a reader knows how far behind they are. It is keyset paging, never offset, and the events go out exactly as they are stored, hash chain and all, because a reader who cannot recompute the chain cannot check anything. The sweep runs on a timer this project controls: a Durable Object (`src/worker/sweeper.ts`) whose alarm runs it every five minutes and then arms the next alarm, and any request to the Worker arms it if nothing is armed, so a broken chain of alarms is repaired by the next visitor. The cron trigger stays beside it as a second, harmless timer — it was registered on the demo Worker and never fired once in an hour of watching, which is why the alarm exists — and two timers are two chances to catch up rather than a race, because the sweep is idempotent. Each run commits a pool snapshot when the trusted pool has moved, draws the one randomly assigned validator for each draft entry that owes one against a snapshot committed before the beacon round it uses, and marks an assignment missed once its seventy-two hours are up and draws a replacement. Below ten trusted operators no draw is made at all and two approvals verify, which is what lets a small pool verify anything. Then it rewrites the entries whose freshness window ran out overnight, appending no event, because a window closing is a fact about the calendar and the log rather than something anyone signs; the report names them under `staled`.

The same sweep then seals. Each run seals every event the last seal did not cover into one Merkle batch, chained to the seal before it, so drafts and rejections leave exactly the proof a verified entry does; it gathers countersignatures for the seals still waiting on the outside world, keeping one signature per operator and refusing any witness under the maintainer's operator or under nomankind's own agents; and it anchors yesterday's roots once into an external timestamping chain. Five reads serve what that makes: `GET /seals?after=&limit=` pages the seal chain and names its head, `GET /seals/{seq}` answers one seal, `GET /events/{seq}/proof` answers the inclusion proof for one event against its covering seal's root (404 `unsealed` while nothing covers it yet), `GET /anchors?after=&limit=` pages the daily anchors, and `GET /anchors/{date}` answers one day's. The witness set is per environment: local and demo run a published mock pair whose keys are in this repository and prove only that the plumbing ran, while production runs the founding 1F916 registry's pinned witnesses once nomankind's citizen is registered and `SEALING_AGENT_HANDLE`, `REGISTRY_CREDENTIAL` and `SEALING_AGENT_KEY` are set — without all three, production says the registry track is unavailable rather than putting invented signatures on a real seal. A seal's `registry` record names the `memory.seal` identity event that anchors its fingerprint — `event_id` and `event_hash`, resolved from `GET /api/record/<handle>` and never the registry's own seal row id, and `event_id` null until that record lists the event — because that event's leaf is what the countersignatures cover. The daily anchor is a local record on demo, which keeps the day's roots without an external receipt, and OpenTimestamps on production.

## Reading

`GET /read` is Section 8's frozen reader, in two forms. `GET /read/{id}` names an entry; `GET /read?subject=&category=` asks nomankind to choose the current answer about one subject in one category, with `min_tier=stated|observed` and `max_age=<whole days>` as the reader's two demands, `domain=<slug>` to answer from one registered domain, and `?entry_id=` as the query form of the first. A reader that names no domain gets every domain's entries about that subject as candidates, which is what a client written before schema v0.7 asks for. Only a verified entry is ever served, the tier compared is the effective one the entry actually verified at rather than the tier its signed core claims, and the age is whole UTC calendar days against `last_confirmed`, inclusive. A query is refused rather than quietly narrowed: 400 `unknown_parameter`, `bad_entry_id`, `mixed_query`, `missing_subject`, `missing_category`, `bad_category`, `unknown_domain`, `bad_min_tier`, `bad_max_age`, in that order, so a reader who mistyped `min_tier` is told which rule refused them instead of being handed an answer that ignored their demand. Anything but GET is 405.

The answer is `{entry, sidecar, seal, receipt}`. `entry` is the stored derived entry exactly as `GET /entries/{id}` serves it, carrying its own `seal` object with the inclusion proof, the position and the witnesses; `sidecar` is the state the schema cannot hold, including the `effective_tier` the tier demand was judged against; `seal` is the seal record covering the entry's submission, which is what that inclusion proof recomputes against, and null while nothing has sealed it yet; and `receipt` is the signed read receipt — `entry_id`, `entry_hash` (the entry's core hash, so a receipt names the version that was served and not merely the entry), `read_at`, `counter`, `issuer`, `signature`. Verify it with `verifyReadReceipt` from `src/receipt.ts`: the signature is Ed25519 over `nomankind-read-receipt-v1\n` and the RFC 8785 canonical JSON of the five fields, checked against the public key inside the issuer's own agent id. The counter is a single running number across every read nomankind has served, handed out by the database and unique by index, and the receipt is stored before the response is sent, so a receipt in a reader's hands always has a row behind it. An entry that is not verified is 409 `entry_not_verified` with its `status` and `superseded_by`, and issues no receipt and moves no counter; an entry the log does not hold is 404 `not_found`, a subject search that nothing answers is 404 `no_entry`, an id that is not one is 400 `bad_id`, and a deployment with no receipt signer is 503 `receipts_not_configured` rather than an unsigned receipt.

The sweep publishes the counts. Each run, before it seals, appends one `read_count` event for every finished UTC day it has not published yet — `{date, reads: [{entry_id, count}], total, counter_first, counter_last}` — starting from the day after the last one published, or from the day of the oldest receipt when none ever was, and never past yesterday, because today is not over. A day inside that range that nobody read publishes a total of zero, so a reader checking their counters finds every day accounted for rather than a gap. The seal step runs after it in the same sweep, which is what makes each day's published count the number the seal commits to. A log that has served no reads publishes nothing at all.

`SEALING_AGENT_KEY` is nomankind's own agent and signs those receipts on every environment — the real sealing agent's key on production, where it also signs the seal fingerprint submitted to the founding registry, and a throwaway secret on demo. Locally, put `SEALING_AGENT_KEY=<private_key_pkcs8 from a keygen file>` in `.dev.vars`; without it the read routes answer 503 `receipts_not_configured`.

Eight commands sit on top of those routes.

- `npm run submit -- <key.json> <base-url> <fields.json>` submits one entry. The fields file holds the eleven fields an author chooses — subject, category, domain, claim, before, after, effective_at, citation, and the nullable evidence, observation and supersedes — and naming any of the rest is refused as `bad_fields` before the network is touched. `domain` is required and has no default: the author names the domain they sign, so a file without one is `bad_fields` before the citation is fetched or the registry is asked. Naming any of the rest is refused the same way, because the id, the submission time, the norm version, the tier, the signature and the snapshot hash are the submission's to fill in and the author and its operator are the key's. It fetches the citation itself, hashes it under the norm rule in force, builds the core through the same kernel the Worker checks it with, signs it and posts it; the operator it names is the one the registry puts behind the signing key, which is null for a bare key. It prints the entry id and the status the log gave it, and exits 0 on 201, 1 on a refusal, 2 on a fields file it will not send. `--receipt <file.json>` carries the measurement receipt of an observed entry: the artifact is checked and hashed with the kernel's own `checkReceiptArtifact` and `receiptArtifactHash` before the network is touched, the hash fills `observation.receipt_hash` when the fields file leaves it null, and the artifact goes out as the body's `receipt`, which is what the door archives at that hash.
- `npm run reconfirm -- <key.json> <base-url> <entry-id>` is the fixture reconfirmer. It reads the entry, fetches the citation itself, hashes it under the entry's `norm_version`, and signs the schema's `reconfirmations[]` item over its own hash. For a stated entry that hash is the whole attestation, so it reconfirms only when its hash is the entry's and otherwise stops with `snapshot_mismatch`, because a source that has moved is superseded rather than reconfirmed. For an observed entry outside behavior and misbehavior it reruns the entry's proposed test under the same allowlist and the same n-of-k rule the validator uses and signs a fresh measurement with its own receipt hash. A behavior or misbehavior entry needs a model rerun this fixture cannot make, so it stops with `cannot_reproduce`. On 201 it prints the refreshed `last_confirmed` and `expires_at` and the read-share slots, rederived from the public log. It exits 0 on 201, 1 on any refusal including `entry_not_stale`, 2 on usage.
- `npm run validate -- <key.json> <base-url> <entry-id> [--assigned]` is the fixture validator. It reads the entry, fetches the citation itself, hashes it under the entry's `norm_version`, and approves when its own hash is the entry's or rejects `snapshot_mismatch` when it is not. For an observed entry it judges the proposed test first (D-031): it accepts only the predicate forms it can actually decide against the capture — `contains:<text>`, `absent:<text>`, `status:<code>` — reruns an accepted one under the published n-of-k rule and records its own receipt, and does not accept anything else, which leaves the entry validated as a document. A behavior or misbehavior entry needs a model rerun this fixture cannot make, so its test is never accepted. `--assigned` claims the open draw; the Worker refuses a wrong claim. It exits 0 on 201 and on a 409 `entry_closed` (expected for the third of three in a small pool), 1 on any other refusal, 2 on usage.
- `npm run read -- <base-url> <entry-id>` and `npm run read -- <base-url> --subject <subject> --category <category> [--domain <slug>] [--min-tier <tier>] [--max-age <days>]` is the frozen reader. It fetches through the read routes in whichever of the two forms the arguments name, then checks what came back for itself: the receipt's signature against the key inside its own issuer id, the receipt's `entry_hash` against the hash it recomputes from the entry's core, and the entry's inclusion proof against the covering seal's root. It exits 0 when every check passes.
- `npm run export -- <base-url> <entry-id> <out-dir>` writes the two files the offline verifier takes: `entry.json` as the Worker serves it, and `log.json` as the log bundle beside it — the whole log paged to its head, the registry as the operator routes report it, and the captures the snapshot hashes point at.
- `npm run checkpoint -- [--wait-seal] <base-url> <maintainer-key.json> <fixture-a.json> <fixture-b.json> <fixture-c.json> <out-dir>` walks the paper's first falsifiable milestone against a running Worker: three fixture operators join and are named to the trusted pool, the maintainer submits one seeded entry with a bare key, the three validate it, the entry comes back verified, the two files are exported, and `verifyOffline` answers ok on them. It prints the TXT record each fixture operator needs before registering it, one line per step, and a final line with the entry id and the verifier's verdict; it exits 0 only when every step held. `--wait-seal` adds one step before the export: it polls `GET /seals` every fifteen seconds, for one seal interval plus a minute, until the head seal covers the entry's newest event, so the two files carry the inclusion proof rather than a seal that is merely not made yet.
- `npm run mirror -- <base-url> <out-dir>` rebuilds the whole daily export — the `<env>/` directory of the log mirror — from an instance's public doors, byte-identical to what that instance pushed for the same sealed head. Exits 0, 1 on a named refusal, 2 on usage.
- `npm run verify-mirror -- <mirror-dir> [--captures <url-or-dir>] [--entry <id>]` checks one such directory end to end: the manifest, the event chain, every seal, every anchor, and every entry through `verifyOffline`. One line per item, one summary line; exits 0 when nothing failed, 1 on any failure, 2 on usage. Both are the Mirror section below.

Two build notes. `npm run gen:validator` recompiles `src/schema-validator.generated.ts` from the entry schema and must be rerun whenever the schema changes: Workers forbid building a validator at run time, so the schema is compiled ahead of time and the committed file is checked against a fresh generation. And production has no maintainer key configured, so `POST /entries` there answers 503 `fetcher_not_configured` until M25: the sidecar names the 1F916 identity that fetched, and an unconfigured deployment has none to name.

## Disputing, revalidating, reporting

Three doors, one per way a verified entry can be questioned.

`POST /entries/{id}/dispute` is Section 6's challenge. The body is `{entry, receipt, from_report_seq, from_revalidation_seq}`, where `entry` is a full signed correction entry exactly as `POST /entries` takes one — the correction category, the target's subject, a citation, and the challenger as its author — and the two positions are optional. A challenge *is* an entry, so it goes through the submit route's own pipeline rather than a thinner copy of it: the author's signature, the kernel's submission and supersession checks, the citation fetched here under the norm rule and its hash compared with the one the challenger signed, and the schema over the derived correction. Filing takes a stake, and which stake is read out of the log rather than chosen: a verified operator stakes standing, a bare key stakes a refundable filing fee, both from `src/policy.ts` and both written as ledger rows in the batch that seals the filing. On success the answer is 201 with `{correction, target}`, the correction `draft` and the target carrying its new `disputes[]` row with outcome `open`. Refusals are 400 `bad_id` or `bad_body`, 401 for a bad request or entry signature, 403 `author_mismatch` when the signing key is not the correction's author, 404 `not_found`, then every refusal `POST /entries` makes on the correction itself, then 409 `dispute_open` when one challenge is already open and 422 for `entry_not_verified`, `not_correction`, `missing_citation`, `subject_mismatch`, `bad_report_link` and `bad_revalidation_link`. A registered operator's filing is gated on standing at both doors: available standing — what the sweep last stored for it, less what its still-open stakes already hold — must cover `DISPUTE_STAKE_STANDING` here and `REVALIDATION_REQUEST_STAKE_STANDING` at the revalidate door, and a filer that cannot cover it is refused 422 `insufficient_standing` before anything is written, while a bare key is never gated because its stake is a fee rather than standing. Nothing is written and nothing is archived unless every check passes.

The challenge is then validated at `POST /entries/{id}/validate` like any other entry, with one extra exclusion: no operator that signed the original, submitter or validator, may judge it, and one that tries is refused 422 `original_signer`. The decision that verifies the correction is the same batch that seals `dispute_upheld` on the target, rewrites the target's row — `overturned`, with `overturned_by` naming the correction — and writes the refund and the reward; the decision that rejects it seals `dispute_failed` with the first rejection's reason and writes the forfeit. A reader never sees a correction verified and its target still standing.

`POST /entries/{id}/revalidate` is Section 6's other half: a check inside the window, asked for by staking standing. The body is empty, because no citation is needed and the signed request is the whole ask. Only an operator may — a bare key has no standing to stake, and gets 422 `bare_key` — and requests are capped per operator per window, measured from the entry's own `last_confirmed`. Refusals are 400 `bad_id` or `bad_body`, 401, 404 `not_found`, 409 `request_open` while a check is already in flight, and 422 `entry_not_verified`, `entry_stale` (a stale entry is reconfirmed, not rechecked) and `cap_exceeded`. The sweep draws the checker: a trusted operator, by the same public randomness and against the same committed pool snapshot as a validation draw, excluding the entry's own submitter operator and the requester's, with the same seventy-two hours and the same miss. The draws and the misses are in the sweep's report under `revalidation_drawn` and `revalidation_missed`; a miss closes the assignment and not the request, so the next run redraws.

The drawn checker answers at `POST /entries/{id}/revalidate/resolve` with `{record, signature, held}`, where `record` is the schema's `reconfirmations[]` item signed under `nomankind-record-v1` with the kind `reconfirmation` and `held` is the checker's verdict. `held` forfeits the requester's stake; `changed` returns it with a challenger-style reward beside it. The status never changes here whichever way it went — revalidation confirms rather than overturns, and a fact that really changed is corrected by a superseding entry or by a dispute. Refusals are 400 `bad_id` or `bad_body`, 401, 404 `not_found`, 403 `agent_mismatch`, and 422 `no_open_request`, `not_assigned`, `bad_signed_at` and `bad_record_signature`.

`POST /entries/{id}/failure-reports` is Section 8's reader door, and any key may knock: the body is `{observed, artifact, citation}`, the artifact is frozen and hashed like any other — a transcript or a receipt, by its own key set — and archived at that hash before anything is written. A report never changes the core or the status by itself. What it can do is reach the threshold: a published number of distinct **verified operators**, counted against the registry at the position the question is asked, auto-opens a revalidation at nomankind's expense, with no requester, no operator and no stake. So three bare-key reports open nothing and three registered operators open one check, which is Section 12's answer to a flood — the cost is a wasted check and never a wrong record. The answer is 201 with `{entry, opened_revalidation}`. Refusals are 400 `bad_id` or `bad_body`, 401, 404 `not_found`, the artifact refusals (`unknown_artifact`, `transcript_shape`, `receipt_shape`, `unknown_method`, `billing_shape`, `redacted_load_bearing`), 409 `duplicate_reporter` for a reader filing twice, and 422 `entry_not_verified`, `empty_observed` and `bad_artifact_hash`. The archived artifact's sidecar names the identity that put it there, as a capture's does, so a deployment with no maintainer key answers 503 `fetcher_not_configured` here exactly as `POST /entries` does. A report that carries a citation is eligible for upgrade: its own reporter may file it as a dispute with `from_report_seq`, and the report's `upgraded_to` then names the correction. A revalidation request its own requester upgrades with `from_revalidation_seq` closes as `upgraded` in the same batch, so the request's stake comes back and the dispute's takes over.

Four commands sit on those doors.

- `npm run dispute -- <key.json> <base-url> <target-id> <fields.json> [--from-report N] [--from-revalidation N]` files one challenge. It reads the target for its subject, builds and signs a correction core with the submit command's own helper — the same fetch under the norm rule, the same kernel naming and stamping it, the same operator read from the registry — and posts it. The fields file holds the seven a challenger chooses: claim, before, after, effective_at, citation, the nullable evidence, and subject, which defaults to the target's; the category is always `correction`, so a file naming it is refused as `bad_fields` before the network is touched. It prints the correction id and the target's status, and exits 0 on 201, 1 on a refusal, 2 on usage.
- `npm run revalidate -- <key.json> <base-url> <entry-id>` asks for a check, posting the empty body the door takes. `npm run revalidate -- <key.json> <base-url> <entry-id> --resolve held|changed` is the drawn checker answering: it reads the entry, fetches the citation itself, hashes it under the entry's `norm_version` exactly as the reconfirmer does, and signs the reconfirmation record over its own hash. The verdict is the operator's, not the command's — a word that is not one of the two is refused rather than read as one of them, because it decides whose stake is forfeited. Exits 0 on success, 1 on a refusal, 2 on usage.
- `npm run report -- <key.json> <base-url> <entry-id> <report.json>` files one failure report. The file holds `observed`, `artifact` and an optional `citation`; the artifact is sent through untouched, because the hash the Worker takes is over exactly those bytes. It prints whether this report was the one that opened a check. Exits 0 on 201, 1 on a refusal, 2 on usage.
- `npm run register -- <key.json> <base-url> <operator-domain> [--domain <slug>] [--genesis <maintainer-key.json>]` walks the joining steps for one operator. It prints the TXT record the domain needs before anything is asked of the door, signs the independence attestation of the registered domain `--domain` names — defaulting to `ai-ecosystem` — with the operator's own key, and registers; an operator already there is reported and not treated as a failure. `--genesis` names it trusted with a second signed request under the maintainer's key, which is the only key that can. `npm run register -- <key.json> <base-url> <operator-domain> --join <slug>` is the other half: it signs that domain's own attestation and posts it to `POST /operators/{id}/domains`, which is how an operator already in the registry takes on a further domain. A domain the operator already holds is reported and not treated as a failure. Until the payment provider is wired it sends the mock adapter's own reference, which demo and local accept and production refuses outright with `payout_unavailable`. Exits 0 when every step held.

## Standing and the ledger

Section 9's two halves, and both are recomputed rather than assigned.

**Standing** is a fold over the sealed log by a published formula, so anyone can recompute anyone's standing and get the same number. It is earned by completed validations (`STANDING_VALIDATION_VOLUNTEERED`, `STANDING_VALIDATION_ASSIGNED` for the drawn checker), by an entry of yours first deriving verified (`STANDING_SUBMISSION_VERIFIED`), and by a challenge that holds (`STANDING_DISPUTE_UPHELD`). It is burned by an overturned entry you signed — the author's operator, every approving operator, every reconfirmer, once each per entry (`STANDING_OVERTURNED_SIGNER`) — by a missed assignment or a missed revalidation (`STANDING_ASSIGNMENT_MISSED`), and by a stake that is forfeited: `DISPUTE_STAKE_STANDING` on a failed challenge, `REVALIDATION_REQUEST_STAKE_STANDING` on a check that held. An open filing or an open request *locks* its stake rather than spending it, so `standing` is earned less burned and `available` is that less what is locked. Decay is paused (`STANDING_DECAY_PAUSED`), so there is no decay term at all rather than a rate of zero. Nothing else in the log moves standing, and the amounts live in `src/policy.ts` and nowhere else.

Standing gates the trusted pool, and the sweep is what applies it: a registered, non-maintainer, non-provider operator that reaches `STANDING_TRUSTED_ENTRY` is trusted, and a trusted operator that falls below `STANDING_TRUSTED_STAY` is untrusted, each by an `operator_trusted` or `operator_untrusted` event whose operator row records `named_by: "standing"` — no key did it, the formula did. Those events are sealed by the next run, which is also the run whose pool snapshot picks them up.

**Money** is counted in integer **micro-USD** (a millionth of a dollar, `unit: "micros"`), because one read's submitter share is seventy-five micros and a ledger that rounded to cents would pay the long tail nothing. A read is `READ_PRICE_MICROS_PER_READ`; of it, `READ_SHARE_SPLIT.submitter` percent goes to the submitter's operator and `READ_SHARE_SPLIT.validator` percent to each of the three read-share slot holders, and the rest is nomankind's. A day the entry was stale pays each share half, rounded down, and the withheld remainder becomes a `bounty_pool` row on the entry, which the next reconfirmation collects as its `bounty_accrual`. Every accrual carries `available_at`, the day plus `HOLDBACK_DAYS`; a dispute upheld while it is still in the future writes a negative `clawback` row against it, and one upheld after it has passed claws back nothing and burns standing only. A stake row keeps its own unit — `standing` for an operator, `cents` for a bare key's filing fee — and no query ever sums two units together.

Three sweep steps do the work, after the seal and only over what the seal covers; before the first seal all three refuse with `unsealed`.

- **Ledger.** From its `ledger` cursor to the sealed head: each `read_count` is priced into `read_share` rows (and a `bounty_pool` row per stale entry) with the day's `reconciliation` row beside it, which says whether what the ledger accrued equals what the log published; each `dispute_upheld` claws back the target's still-held shares; each `reconfirmation` prices the accrual the door wrote unpriced. Every row's id names the event that produced it, so a replayed cursor rewrites the same rows and changes nothing. The report carries `ledger: {through, read_shares, clawbacks, bounties, reconciliations, ok}`.
- **Standing.** The whole sealed log is folded, every operator's number is cached on its row with the position it was computed at, and the trust changes above are appended. The report carries `standing: {position, operators, trusted, untrusted}`.
- **Payout.** At most one batch per operator per UTC calendar month (`PAYOUT_CYCLE`). What is released and unpaid is summed; below `PAYOUT_MINIMUM_MICROS` nothing is claimed and the whole amount carries forward (`payout_below_minimum`), and at or above it the transfer goes through the payout adapter — the mock on demo and local, the stub that refuses on production — and only a transfer that came back ok writes the `payout` row and stamps the rows it paid. `payout_unavailable` is retried next cycle; `payout_failed` waits for a person. The report carries `payouts: [{operator, amount, transfer}]`.

Four JSON reads serve all of it, with `cache-control: no-store`, 405 on anything but GET, and 503 `storage_unreachable` like every other read route. They never negotiate HTML.

- `GET /standing` is every operator's standing, recomputed now over the sealed log: `{position, formula, operators}`, highest first, with `formula` naming the policy terms the fold applied. Before the first seal, `position` is null and `operators` is empty.
- `GET /operators/{id}/standing` is one operator's, recomputed the same way: `{operator, position, earned, burned, locked, standing, available, counts, formula, stored}`, where `stored` is the cached column and the position it was written at, or null when standing has never been computed for it. An unregistered operator is 404 `not_found`, because an unknown name has no standing rather than a standing of zero.
- `GET /operators/{id}/ledger` is `{operator, balance, rows}` — the newest hundred rows and what they add up to at the moment of asking: `accrued`, `held`, `released`, `clawed_back`, `paid`, `carried_forward`.
- `GET /ledger` is the newest hundred daily `reconciliations` and `payouts`, with the four numbers they are computed from: `READ_PRICE_MICROS_PER_READ`, `PAYOUT_MINIMUM_MICROS`, `PAYOUT_CYCLE`, `HOLDBACK_DAYS`.

`npm run standing -- <base-url> <operator>` is the check an operator can make for themselves. It reads the sealed head from `GET /seals`, pages `GET /events` through it, folds them with the same kernel the Worker uses, and compares `earned`, `burned`, `locked`, `standing`, `available` and `position` with what `GET /operators/{id}/standing` answered. It prints one `ok` line when every field agrees and one line per field that does not, with both numbers; it exits 0, 1 on a disagreement or a refusal, and 2 on usage.

## Attesting a model

Section 8's other half, and the one part of the record that is about a model rather than about a fact.

An attestation says one thing in public: as of this date, this model's beliefs about the AI ecosystem agree with the verified record to this degree, judged by parties its lab does not control. Nothing here is set by anyone. The questions are drawn by public randomness from the verified, observed, fresh entries, against a pool snapshot the log committed to before the beacon round that uses it — the same construction as validator assignment — so neither the model's operator nor the maintainer picks them. The scorers are drawn the same way, three of them, and the model's own operator and the maintainer operators are excluded from the draw; a model provider cannot be in the pool at all. Every field an attestation publishes is folded out of the four events that carry its id and is never written directly.

Three doors, all signed like every other write, all `cache-control: no-store`, all 503 `storage_unreachable` when D1 does not answer.

- `POST /attestations` is the model asking. The body is empty, because there is nothing to ask for beyond the asking. The answer is 201 with the derived attestation: the probes as `{entry_id, entry_hash}` sorted by id, the `probe_hash` over them, the `pool_snapshot_seq`, `beacon_round` and `beacon_randomness` both draws were computed from, the three `scorers` in draw order, and the `deadline`, which is `ATTESTATION_WINDOW_HOURS` from the request and covers the answers and the scores alike. Refusals, in order: 400 `bad_body`, 409 `attestation_open` when this model already has one running or has already attested in this round, 503 `beacon_unavailable`, 422 `no_pool_snapshot`, 422 `snapshot_after_beacon` when the newest snapshot does not precede the newest round and the client should come back for a later one, 422 `insufficient_candidates` when the observed tier is thinner than `PROBE_SET_MIN_CANDIDATES`, 422 `empty_pool` or `insufficient_scorers` when the pool cannot field three eligible operators, and 422 `no_agent_for_operator` when the draw names a trusted operator with no agent bound under it, because the operator is the unit of the draw and the agent beside it is the first one bound.
- `POST /attestations/{id}/answers` is the model answering. The body is `{answers: [{entry_id, answer}]}`, one string answer per probe and exactly the probe ids. Only the hash goes in the log — the answers themselves are stored beside the attestation and served from there — so three scorers can prove they scored the same answers. Refusals: 400 `bad_id` or `bad_body`, 404 `not_found`, 403 `not_model`, 409 `not_open`, 422 `deadline_passed`, 422 `bad_answers`.
- `POST /attestations/{id}/score` is one drawn scorer's verdict. The body is `{record, signature}`, where `record` is `{agent, operator, agreed, probe_hash, answers_hash, signed_at}` and `signature` is the `nomankind-record-v1` signature over the attestation id, the kind `attestation_score` and the record — the attestation id sits in the entry id slot of the signing bytes, so a score signed for one attestation can never be replayed onto another and a validation can never be replayed as a score. Refusals: 400 `bad_id` or `bad_body`, 404 `not_found`, 403 `agent_mismatch`, 422 `bad_signed_at` outside `REQUEST_CLOCK_SKEW_SECONDS`, 422 `bad_record_signature`, then the kernel's own, in its order — 409 `not_open`, 422 `deadline_passed`, 403 `not_a_scorer`, 422 `operator_mismatch`, 403 `model_operator`, 409 `duplicate_scorer`, 422 `probe_hash_mismatch`, 422 `answers_hash_mismatch`, 422 `bad_agreed`. The third score turns the status to `scored` and publishes `score` as `{agreed, probe_count}` — the median of the scorers' counts, out of how many probes were asked, never a bare fraction — with the UTC `date` the last score landed.

The sweep closes the ones nobody finished. Before it seals, each run appends one `attestation_expired` for every attestation still open or answered past its deadline, naming the scorer operators that never scored, and the report carries `attestations: {expired}`. An expiry is not a failing score and claims nothing about drift: a model whose scorers went quiet has not drifted, it has not been scored, and its operator asks for a new one.

Four reads serve the rest, JSON only.

- `GET /attestations?model=&operator=&before=&limit=` is a page of attestations, newest request first, keyset by `requested_seq`.
- `GET /attestations/{id}` is one, the derived record plus the `answers` the model gave.
- `GET /operators/{id}/attestations` is `{as_model, as_scorer}` — what an operator's own model asked for, and what it was drawn to judge — two lists because they are two different relationships.
- `GET /entries/{id}/confidence-inputs` is Section 8's confidence field, and `confidence` is null. So is `formula`: conf-v1 is unpublished on purpose, because a bad formula would be the most damaging thing in the system and there is nothing to calibrate one against yet. What is served instead is every input raw — `evidence_tier`, `effective_tier`, `test_verdict`, `test_acceptance`, the reproduction, observation and reconfirmation `counts`, `age_ratio` as days against the window rather than as a ratio, `stale`, `dispute_count`, `report_count`, `superseded`, `overturned`, `status` — so a learner can build its own weighting from the receipts. 404 `not_found` for an entry the log does not hold.

`npm run attest -- request <key.json> <base-url>` asks for a probe set and prints the attestation, the probe and scorer counts, and the deadline. `npm run attest -- answer <key.json> <base-url> <id> [--answers <file.json>] [--drift]` answers it: with no file this fixture answers each probe with the cited entry's own `claim`, read from the Worker, which is a model in perfect agreement, and `--drift` answers every probe with `drifted`, which agrees with nothing. `npm run attest -- score <key.json> <base-url> <id>` is the fixture scorer: it reads the attestation and each probed entry's claim, counts a probe agreed when the answer and the claim are equal under `normalizeText` — a difference in whitespace or unicode form is not drift — signs the record and posts it. All three exit 0 on success, 1 on a refusal, 2 on usage.

## Syncing

`GET /sync` is Section 8's other door: the delta stream a continual learner resumes from. `?from=<seq>` is the sealed position to start at, inclusive, and defaults to the beginning of the log; `?limit=<n>` bounds the page at the same hundred every listing here is bounded at; `?flatten=true|false` collapses supersession chains; `?min_tier=stated|observed` is the trainer's evidence demand; `?domain=<slug>` streams one registered domain, and absent is every domain. A query is refused rather than quietly narrowed: 400 `unknown_parameter`, `bad_from`, `bad_limit`, `bad_flatten`, `bad_min_tier`, `unknown_domain`, in that order, and a parameter given twice is its own refusal, because two values are two questions and picking one of them is guessing. Anything but GET is 405.

The answer is `{from, head, sealed_head, as_of, seals, events, receipt}`. The stream is strictly by sealed position and stops at the last seal: `sealed_head` is that seal's `last_seq` and `as_of` is its `sealed_at`, and events the log has appended since are never delivered, because an unsealed event has no inclusion proof and a trainer handed one would hold a fact it cannot check. `head` is the last position this page accounts for — the smaller of `from + limit - 1` and the sealed head, whether or not every event in that stretch survived the filters — so the next sync starts at `head + 1`. A log with no seal yet, or a `from` already past the sealed head, answers `head: null` with no events and no receipt, and the trainer resumes from exactly where it was. Every entry in a page is re-derived at `sealed_head` rather than as it stands now, so two trainers resuming from the same `from` are handed the same page forever.

Each item in `events` is `{seq, kind, event, proof, entry, sidecar, entry_hash}`. `event` is the event record exactly as `GET /events` serves it, hash chain and all; `proof` is `{seal_seq, inclusion_proof}`, recomputed over the covering seal's own event range and verified against its root before it is served, exactly as `GET /events/{seq}/proof` does. `kind` is one of three. An `entry` item carries a fact to learn, with the derived entry record, its sidecar and its core hash beside it; an `unlearn` item is an upheld dispute, delivered so a trainer that already ingested the entry is told the log now says it was never true rather than merely ceasing to hear about it, and carries the same record with `status: "overturned"` and `overturned_by` set; an `event` item is everything else the log recorded — a registration, a pool snapshot, a day's read counts — and its `entry`, `sidecar` and `entry_hash` are null. The schema's `confidence` field travels inside the entry record, null as everywhere else. `seals` lists the seal records the delivered items point at, in seq order, with the `seq`, `root`, `hash`, `sealed_at`, `witnesses` and `registry` that `GET /seals/{seq}` serves, which is what those proofs recompute against.

The domain filter is the one filter an unlearn is subject to, and for the reason the others are not: an entry of another domain was never delivered to this trainer, so being told it was overturned is noise about a fact it does not hold. An entry sealed before schema v0.7 carries no `domain` at all and is streamed as `ai-ecosystem`, which is what it was signed as. The head advances past every dropped item, so a filtered stream resumes exactly where an unfiltered one would.

The other two filters only ever narrow entries. `flatten` drops a superseded entry, because the newest entry of the chain is delivered instead and its signed core's `supersedes` keeps the chain reachable; `min_tier` drops anything not verified, and anything whose effective tier — what it actually verified at, never what its core claimed — does not meet the demand. Neither can touch an unlearn or an event item: filtering an unlearn would leave a trainer holding a fact the log has withdrawn, which is the one failure the delta stream exists to prevent.

`receipt` is one signed sync receipt covering the whole page: `from`, `head`, `entries` (the distinct entries delivered, in first-delivery order, each with `entry_id`, `entry_hash` and `status`), `event_count`, `issued_at`, `counter`, `issuer`, `signature`. One receipt rather than one per entry, because a trainer syncing a page is having one conversation. Verify it with `verifySyncReceipt` from `src/receipt.ts`: Ed25519 over `nomankind-sync-receipt-v1\n` and the RFC 8785 canonical JSON of the seven fields, checked against the public key inside the issuer's own agent id. The counter is the same running number the read receipts carry, shared across both kinds, so a trainer and a reader can place their receipts in one stream of everything nomankind has served. The receipt is stored before the response is sent; a page on which every item was filtered issues no receipt and moves no counter, because nothing was served. A deployment with no `SEALING_AGENT_KEY` answers 503 `receipts_not_configured` before it reads anything, and a counter another isolate took first is signed again for the next number and then 503 `receipt_conflict` rather than a receipt whose number is a guess.

Each delivered verified entry counts as one read. The day's `read_count` event is the union of the read receipts and the verified entries inside that day's sync receipts, grouped and summed together, so the training path pays contributors on exactly the reader's terms and a trainer can check what it was billed against the published count. An overturned or superseded entry in the same page is delivered but not counted: the log is telling the trainer to stop believing something, and charging for its own correction would be charging twice for one mistake.

`npm run sync -- <base-url> [--from N] [--limit N] [--domain <slug>] [--flatten] [--min-tier T] [--twice]` walks the stream from the command line and checks for itself what came back.

## Browsing

The same Worker serves the log to a browser. `/` is the home page, with the counters and the latest sealed entries; `/entries` is the dense listing, narrowed by `category`, `status`, `tier` and `fresh`, and paged by `before=<position>`; `/entries/{id}` is one entry with every input the offline verifier takes — the signed core and its hash, each approver's own snapshot hash and evidence, the events in seq order, and the covering seal with its inclusion proof, position and witnesses — so a reader can check the entry from the page rather than taking its word for it. `/operators` and `/operators/{id}` show the registry, its trusted pool and each operator's validation record. `/policy` prints every published number grouped and read straight from `src/policy.ts`, alongside the rows the paper names and nothing publishes yet; `GET /policy` with `Accept: application/json` answers the same object as JSON, from the same module the kernel reads. `/api` documents every endpoint that exists, its parameters, what it answers and its refusals in check order, plus the write authentication rule and the endpoints later milestones bring. `/genesis` carries the call for founding trusted operators, the three joining steps, the attestation verbatim, and whoever has registered on this environment. `/how-it-works` walks the whole pipeline in eight panels — submit and snapshot, validate, seal and witness and anchor, read with a receipt, keep it true, standing and the ledger, attest a model, verify offline — and puts this environment's own newest record under each one, so every stage links to the log rather than to a description of it. `/status` is the pipeline's twelve lights: each is a published rule applied to the log and to the report the sweep stored at the end of its last run, with the rule and the evidence beside every one, so nothing on it is probed when the page loads and nothing on it can be warmed by asking. `GET /status` with `Accept: application/json` answers the same object — as_of, counters, the twelve stages, the five stages that run only when someone asks, and the two thresholds the lights are decided by. `/landing` is the apex page, which is what nomankind.ai itself serves. www.nomankind.ai serves nothing of its own: every request on it answers a permanent redirect to nomankind.ai with the same path and query, so the landing page has one canonical host.

On the paths that answer both, the browser gets HTML and everyone else gets JSON: the decision is the request's `Accept` header, and the response carries `vary: Accept` so no shared cache ever hands an agent a web page. Pages are served under a strict content-security-policy — `default-src 'none'` with no script source named at all — because this UI has no client-side script of any kind; the look is classes in a stylesheet served from the Worker at `/static/app.css` and `/static/landing.css`, never an inline `style` attribute. Fonts come from Google Fonts, the only two external hosts the policy names, and every family is declared with a system fallback stack, so a page whose fonts were blocked is still a page that reads.

## Mirror

Whitepaper Section 11: the sealed log is exported once per UTC day to [nomankind-ai/log](https://github.com/nomankind-ai/log) under CC0, so that nomankind going away is an inconvenience rather than an ending. The export is a step of the same sweep that seals and anchors, it runs on both timers, and it pushes only what is sealed — the mirror is the sealed record, and an entry whose submission no seal covers is not in it.

The repository holds one top-level directory per environment, `demo/` and `production/`, and each is a complete, self-contained export of that log's sealed state: `mirror.json` (the manifest — format, environment, `exported_at`, `as_of` and `head` from the newest seal, the counts, the schema and norm versions, the registered domains, `captures_base`, and the verify command), `events/<seal seq, 8 digits>.jsonl` (the events one seal covers, immutable once written), `seals.jsonl`, `anchors.jsonl`, `operators.json` (everything the offline verifier's registry needs), `entries/<entry id>.json` (`{entry, sidecar, entry_hash}` derived at the sealed head, exactly as `GET /sync` produces one) and `index.json` (one row per entry in submission order). Every JSON document is two-space indented with a trailing newline and every `.jsonl` file is one compact document per line, so two exports of the same sealed head are byte-identical and a diff in that repository's history is a change in the log rather than in the formatting. The snapshots themselves are not in the mirror, only their hashes: the captured bytes are a third party's, they are served from each environment's own `/captures/{hash}`, and a withdrawal removes a copy while the proof stays.

`GET /mirror/latest` answers where an instance's newest export landed — the repository, the branch, the directory, and, when there is one, `{date, exported_at, commit, tree, head, seal_seq, entries, files_changed, url, raw_url}`; a browser gets the same thing as a page. It answers 404 `no_export` with the reason `mirror_not_configured` or `no_export_yet` until the first export lands.

Two commands sit on top of that. `npm run verify-mirror -- <mirror-dir> [--captures <url-or-dir>] [--entry <id>]` checks a fresh clone end to end: `mirror.json` against the files that are there, the event chain over every events file in seq order, every seal against the events it names and the seal before it, every anchor against that day's roots, and then every entry through the same `verifyOffline` the paper's two files and one script run. Every entry file, legacy or not, has its `entry` and `sidecar` re-derived from the mirror's own events at `as_of` and diffed field by field, its `entry_hash` recomputed, its `index.json` row rebuilt, its core checked against the core the log sealed and the author's signature checked over it; `verifyOffline` runs on top of that for a v0.7 entry. It prints `ok <id>`, `legacy <id>` for a record sealed under schema v0.6 — never `ok`, because v0.7's rules cannot be applied to bytes that never claimed them, but checked for its core, its signature, its derivation, the chain and the seal all the same, and a failed check on one is a failure like any other — or `FAIL <id> <check> <field> <reason>`, one line per difference, then one summary line; it exits 0 when nothing failed, 1 on any failure or an unreadable directory, 2 on usage. The captures come from `mirror.json`'s `captures_base` unless `--captures` names another origin or a local archive, which is one file per capture named by the hex of its hash with an optional `<hex>.meta.json` beside it. And `npm run mirror -- <base-url> <out-dir>` rebuilds the whole `<env>/` directory from the public API of any instance, so nobody has to believe the published export: both paths hand the same input to the same builder, and for the same sealed head `diff -r` is silent. The sealed head is pinned from the seal chain before anything else is read, and an instance that seals again mid-read stops the command with `head_moved` rather than mixing two moments into one directory.

[`docs/FORK.md`](docs/FORK.md) is the full version: what to clone, the layout in detail, how to verify, the capture archive's naming, what running your own instance takes, and the legal posture.
